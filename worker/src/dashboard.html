All checked devices

Every device in this selection, split four ways: traces of the attack of 20 November only, the older backdoor only, both, or no known trace. The four parts add up to the whole.

How an attack ran

    Clean on a later check

    Devices that sent a report with traces of the attack and checked again later, told apart by their device ID (script 2.9 and later). The newest report of each device decides: clean, or traces still there.

    “After a firmware change”: the newest report comes from other firmware than the last one with traces, mostly an update. Firmware with GL.iNet’s cleanup removes the known traces at its first start. “Same firmware”: cleaned some other way, for example with the script’s --fix. The older backdoor is not counted here.

    Traces after cleaning

    Does cleaning hold? Two ways to see it. The older backdoor is not counted here.

    “After a clean check”: a device sent a clean report and later one with traces, told apart by its device ID (script 2.9 and later). If a trace carries a time after the clean check, something wrote it since: the device was hit again, or the malware started again. If all its times lie before the clean check, that check missed them, mostly because a newer script finds more; the note counts those.

    “On firmware with GL.iNet’s cleanup”: a report with traces from firmware that removes the known traces at its first start. Malware or a remote shell there means the cleanup missed them, or the device was hit again. The cleanup deletes the malware’s folder and its lines in rc.local and crontab; the state file of the cloud command is not on its list, so that trace from before the update can stay.

    Checked per day

    The reports by the day they came in, UTC. With “One row per device”, a device counts once a day, with its report that has the most traces; a device that checks on several days counts on each of them.

    The lower chart gives two shares of each day: the devices with traces of 20 November, and those that run firmware with GL.iNet’s cleanup. A falling first line and a rising second one say the update arrives. Read the first days with care: whoever suspects something checks first, so the share hit is highest there. Days with fewer than 20 checks are drawn pale.
